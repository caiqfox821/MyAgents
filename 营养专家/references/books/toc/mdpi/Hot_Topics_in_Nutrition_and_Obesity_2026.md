- About the Editors
- Javier Gómez-Ambrosi
-      Javier Gómez-Ambrosi is a researcher at the Metabolic Research Laboratory of the Clínica
- Universidad de Navarra and Associate Professor at the School of Medicine in the University of
- Navarra, Pamplona, Spain. His main area of research is obesity and related morbidities, from
- a clinical and molecular point of view.     His research combines basic research in experimental
- animals and cells with the clinical setting, trying to disentangle the pathophysiological mechanisms
- responsible of the impact of adiposity on the development of comorbidities. He has published more
- than 235 articles (h-index 68) and has been PI in more than 20 research projects.
- Victoria Catalán
-      Victoria Catalán is a researcher at the Metabolic Research Laboratory, Department of
- Endocrinology and Nutrition, Clínica Universidad de Navarra, Pamplona, Spain. Her research
- focuses on obesity and its associated metabolic complications, with particular interest in adipose
- tissue dysfunction, inﬂammation, intestinal barrier integrity and the molecular mechanisms linking
- obesity with insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes. Her work combines clinical studies in patients
- with obesity with experimental approaches using adipose tissue explants, primary cells, cell culture
- models, and animal models of diet-induced obesity and bariatric surgery. She has authored more
- than 160 scientiﬁc publications, with an h index of 58 in Web of Science, and has led and participated
- in numerous competitive research projects focused on obesity, inﬂammation, metabolic disease, and
- gastrointestinal health.

- Preface
-     Obesity remains one of the most important health challenges worldwide and is closely linked
- to metabolic disease, chronic inﬂammation, impaired quality of life, and a growing burden for
- health systems. Its prevention and management require an integrated perspective that considers
- dietary habits, lifestyle, body composition, psychosocial factors, biological mechanisms, and the
- broad clinical consequences of excess adiposity.
