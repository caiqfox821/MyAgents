- 1. Introduction
- The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends a reduction of intake to less than 2 g/day sodium (5 g/day
- salt) in adults to reduce blood pressure and the risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD), stroke and coronary heart
- disease (CHD) (strong recommendation) (1). In 2013, the World Health Assembly endorsed the Global Action
- Plan for the Prevention and Control of Noncommunicable Diseases 2013–2020.1 The overarching goal of the
- Global Action Plan is to reduce the preventable and avoidable burden of morbidity, mortality and disability due
- to noncommunicable disease (NCD) through multisectoral collaboration and cooperation at national, regional
- and global levels. Together with the Global Action Plan, Member States endorsed the nine voluntary global NCD
- targets, one of which was a 30% relative reduction in mean population intake of sodium by 2025 (3).
- The Global Burden of Disease Study 2017 estimates global sodium intake to be 6 g/day (4) – about three times
- higher than the WHO recommendation. Intakes vary in different countries in different regions, with high intakes
- in East Asia, high-income Asia-Pacific countries and high-income areas of North America. Intakes are lower in
- the Caribbean and Central sub-Saharan Africa; however, sodium intakes exceed the WHO recommendation in all
- regions (4).
